Design notes

  • Precise location is useful for turn-by-turn directions, but ask only when needed and explain why.
  • Route histories can reveal routines and sensitive visits.
  • Route-level sharing with advertisers creates a separate privacy risk.

The score is a teaching aid, not a legal verdict. A low score does not replace security, transparency, or meaningful user choice.

Ethics asks more than “Can we?”

Would a reasonable user expect this collection? Is it needed for the feature? Can people understand, refuse, and change their choice?

Start with less

Collect only what the service needs, limit access and retention, explain the purpose, and give people a real choice before optional tracking or sharing.

02 / Read the data trail

One permission can create many signals.

The exact data depends on the app, device, permissions, and settings. A location feature does not automatically collect every item below; check the app's notice and controls.

01 / A location fix

Where and when

A location fix may pair coordinates with a timestamp and an estimate of accuracy. Repeated fixes can show a route, speed, stops, and trip start or end points.

02 / Context

What patterns suggest

Repeated visits can suggest a home, school, workplace, routine, or sensitive destination. These are inferences, not proof; people deserve a chance to understand and challenge consequential profiles.

03 / Reduce exposure

A practical privacy check

  • Allow location only while using navigation when that meets your needs.
  • Turn off background access and precise location when they are not needed.
  • Review saved trips, delete old history, and check retention and sharing settings.
  • Read the notice before installing; avoid sharing an account or route more broadly than needed.
04 / Contracts are not consent

What an NDA does

A non-disclosure agreement is a contract that can restrict how parties use or disclose specified confidential information. It does not itself make tracking ethical, replace a privacy notice or required consent, or cancel legal rights. Check who is bound, what is covered, and the contract's limits.

03 / Law is a floor, not the whole design

Two rules. Two different scopes.

A law can require specific protections in a specific jurisdiction. It does not answer every ethical question about an app's design.

United States · FederalChildren's privacy

COPPA

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and FTC Rule apply to covered online services directed to children under 13, and to general-audience services with actual knowledge they are collecting personal information from a child under 13.

  • Precise geolocation that can identify a street and city or town counts as personal information under the Rule.
  • Covered operators generally must give parents notice and obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting it, unless a specific exception applies.
  • For a child-directed app, turning off location later is not a substitute for required notice and consent before collection.
Source: Federal Trade Commission COPPA FAQ ↗
California · StateConsumer privacy

CCPA, as amended by CPRA

For California residents, the CCPA provides privacy rights when a business is covered by the law. Precise geolocation is an example of sensitive personal information.

  • Rights include knowing, deleting, and correcting personal information.
  • Consumers can opt out of sale or sharing and can limit certain uses of sensitive personal information.
  • Coverage, exceptions, and the details of each right matter; the law does not apply to every business in the same way.
Source: California Attorney General CCPA FAQ ↗

Important: These are classroom summaries, not legal advice. Laws and regulations change; check the official sources for current text and applicability. An NDA can protect confidential information shared in a business relationship, but it does not itself authorize collecting someone's data or erase privacy rights.

06 / Before you tap Allow

Pause. Check the data path.

A permission prompt is only one part of the decision. Check what the feature needs, what the app keeps, and who else can receive the information.

01 / Need

Does this feature need exact location?

Turn-by-turn directions may need precise location while a trip is active. A feature that does not need a live route may not need continuous background access. Ask for the least access that still works.

02 / Keep

What happens after the trip?

Look for saved route history, retention periods, and deletion controls. A location permission and a stored trip history are different things; turning off one may not remove the other.

03 / Share

Who else receives the data?

Read the collection notice and sharing settings. If you are a California resident and the business is covered by the CCPA, review its options for requests to know, delete, and opt out of sale or sharing; exceptions apply.
